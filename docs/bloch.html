Bloch Sphere Quantum Shorts

A glowing 3D Bloch sphere that animates the canonical scenes of quantum computing — ground state, Hadamard, Rabi oscillation, phase gate, measurement collapse, and decoherence. The six scenes

Each scene narrates a key quantum-computing concept. The state vector (coloured arrow) traces a trajectory on the Bloch sphere. Click any scene to jump.

  1. 1Ground state |0⟩
    North pole — the qubit's resting state, classical 0.
  2. 2Hadamard → |+⟩
    Equal superposition (1/√2)(|0⟩ + |1⟩). The Hadamard gate.
  3. 3Rabi oscillation
    Resonant drive; state rotates around Y at the Rabi frequency Ω.
  4. 4Phase gate S
    S|+⟩ = (1/√2)(|0⟩ + i|1⟩) = |+i⟩. Rotates around Z.
  5. 5Measurement collapse
    P(|0⟩) = |α|²; P(|1⟩) = |β|². The state vector jumps.
  6. 6Decoherence
    Bloch vector spirals inward; off-diagonals decay with T2.
  7. 7Bell state |Φ+⟩
    2-qubit entanglement — measuring A determines B instantly. Einstein's "spooky action."
  8. 8Bell singlet |Ψ-⟩
    Anti-correlated in every basis. State used in CHSH tests.
  9. 9CHSH violation
    |S| = 2√2 ≈ 2.83 — exceeds classical bound of 2 (Bell 1964).

Why a Bloch sphere?

The Bloch sphere is the geometric representation of a single qubit's pure state. Every pure state |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ (with |α|² + |β|² = 1) corresponds to a unique point on the unit sphere — parametrised by polar angle θ (population) and azimuthal angle φ (phase). The Bloch sphere makes quantum gates geometric: a single-qubit unitary is a rigid rotation of the sphere.

Mixed states (after decoherence) live inside the sphere — the length of the Bloch vector is the state's purity. Scene 6 shows this shrinkage.
