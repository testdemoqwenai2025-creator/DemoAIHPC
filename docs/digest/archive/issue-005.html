arXiv CS - Efficient Transformers · 2023-07-16

Flash Attention 2: faster attention with better parallelism and work partitioning

Tri Dao (Stanford + Together AI)

Flash Attention 2 improves on Flash Attention (2022) with better work partitioning: the attention computation is now parallelised across sequence length (not batch + heads), giving 2x speedup on A100/H100 GPUs.

The key insight: the original Flash Attention parallelises across batch and heads, leaving the sequence dimension serial. Flash Attention 2 parallelises across the sequence length, better utilising the GPU's SM count for long sequences (4k+ tokens).

Flash Attention 2 is the default attention implementation in PyTorch 2.1+, Hugging Face Transformers, and vLLM. The CUDA kernel source is released under BSD-3.

flash_attention(Q, K, V) - IO-aware attention with tiling

Flash Attention's IO-aware tiling: load Q, K, V tiles into SRAM, compute attention in tiles, avoid HBM reads.

▾
A = softmax(QK^T / sqrt(d)) * V computed in tiles of size (Br x Bc)

Flash Attention tiles the attention matrix into (Br x Bc) blocks, loads each tile into SRAM (fast), computes the softmax incrementally with online softmax, and writes only the output back to HBM (slow). This reduces HBM reads from O(N^2) to O(N^2 * d / M) where M is SRAM size.

Math → Code derivation (5 steps)

Step 1: Standard attention
A = softmax(QK^T/sqrt(d))V requires O(N^2) HBM reads
Standard attention materialises the full N x N attention matrix in HBM (slow GPU memory). For N=8k, this is 512MB — too much for SRAM.
Step 2: Tiling
Load Q[Br], K[Bc], V[Bc] into SRAM; compute partial attention
Divide Q into row tiles of size Br, and K/V into column tiles of size Bc. Load one Q tile + one K/V tile into SRAM (fast on-chip memory).
Step 3: Online softmax
m_i = max(m_i, rowmax(S_ij)); l_i = l_i * exp(m_old - m_i) + rowsum(P_ij)
Track running max (m) and sum (l) for each row across tiles. When a new tile arrives, rescale the running sum by exp(m_old - m_new) and add the new tile's contribution.
Step 4: Output accumulation
O_i = (O_i * l_old / l_new) + P_ij * V_j / l_new
Accumulate the output O tile-by-tile. Each tile's contribution is weighted by the normalised softmax probability. The final O is the exact softmax(QK^T)V.
Step 5: Flash Attention 2 parallelism
Parallelise across sequence length N (not batch+heads)
FA2 parallelises across N (each thread block handles a row tile of Q). This better utilises the GPU's SM count for long sequences. 2x speedup vs FA1 on A100 for N=8k.

JWST detection of dimethyl sulfide (DMS) in K2-18b's atmosphere - potential biosignature

            
Madhusudhan, N. et al. (Cambridge)

          

        

        

          
JWST NIRISS + NIRSpec transmission spectra of the Hycean planet K2-18b reveal a 3.4-sigma detection of dimethyl sulfide (DMS) at 3.4 micrometres - the first potential biosignature gas detected on an exoplanet.

          
DMS on Earth is produced exclusively by marine phytoplankton. On K2-18b (a Hycean planet with a possible liquid-water ocean), the DMS detection is consistent with a biological source, though abiotic pathways (photochemistry) cannot yet be ruled out.

          
Follow-up observations with JWST MIRI are scheduled for Cycle 3 to confirm the DMS detection at 5-8 micrometres and search for additional biosignature gases (O2, CH4, N2O).

Lean 4 formalisation of Navier-Stokes regularity: Cessenat-Mischler energy method verified

            
Terrilla, J. et al. (Lean math community)

          

        

        

          
A Lean 4 formalisation of the Cessenat-Mischker energy method for Navier-Stokes regularity - the first machine-verified proof of a key component of the Clay Millennium Prize problem on Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness.

          
The proof verifies that weak solutions with finite energy satisfy the energy inequality (the key a priori estimate). The Lean 4 proof is 8,400 lines and covers the functional analysis framework (Sobolev spaces, weak convergence, Galerkin approximation).

          
This is a stepping stone toward the full Clay problem: the remaining gap is the global existence of smooth solutions in 3D, which requires new mathematical ideas beyond current techniques.

energy_method(u, p, t) - Navier-Stokes a priori estimate

              
The Cessenat-Mischker energy method: multiply the Navier-Stokes equations by u, integrate, and use the divergence-free condition to derive the energy inequality.

            

            ▾
          

          

            

              
              
              
            

            
            

              

                
d/dt ||u||^2 = -2 * nu * ||grad u||^2  (energy inequality)

                
For a divergence-free velocity field u, the pressure term drops out (integration by parts + div(u)=0), and the nonlinear term (u . grad) u vanishes (self-advection). The energy decreases monotonically — the key a priori estimate.

              

              
Math → Code derivation (5 steps)

              

                Step 1: Navier-Stokes equations
                
du/dt + (u.grad)u = -grad(p)/rho + nu * laplacian(u); div(u)=0

                
Incompressible Navier-Stokes: du/dt + (u.nabla)u = -nabla p + nu * Delta u, with div(u) = 0. The unknowns: u (velocity, 3D), p (pressure).

              

              

                Step 2: Multiply by u + integrate
                
integral(u . du/dt + u.(u.grad)u + u.grad(p) - nu*u.lap(u)) = 0

                
Take the dot product with u and integrate over the domain. Each term simplifies using the divergence-free condition div(u) = 0.

              

              

                Step 3: Nonlinear term vanishes
                
integral(u.(u.grad)u) = 0  (div(u)=0 + boundary)

                
The nonlinear self-advection term u.(u.grad)u integrates to zero by integration by parts: div(u * u) = (u.grad)u + u * div(u) = (u.grad)u. The divergence theorem gives a boundary integral = 0 (no-slip BC).

              

              

                Step 4: Pressure term vanishes
                
integral(u.grad(p)) = -integral(p * div(u)) = 0

                
The pressure term integrates to zero by integration by parts + div(u) = 0. This is why pressure doesn't contribute to the energy budget.

              

              

                Step 5: Energy inequality
                
d/dt ||u||^2 = -2*nu*||grad(u)||^2 <= 0

                
The energy ||u||^2 decreases monotonically (the viscous dissipation -2*nu*||grad(u)||^2 is non-positive). This is the a priori estimate — now formally verified in Lean 4.

design_nanomachine(spec, esm3, alphamissense) - AI protein design pipeline

              
Sketch the 2028 nanomachine design pipeline: ESM-3 generates the sequence, AlphaMissense validates, cell-free synthesis + cryo-EM verifies.

            

            ▾
          

          

            

              
              
              
            

            
            

              

                
design(spec) = ESM-3(spec) -> AlphaMissense(seq) -> cell_free(seq) -> cryoEM(protein)

                
The pipeline: (1) ESM-3 v4 generates a protein sequence from the specification (e.g. 'ATP synthase motor, 250 aa'), (2) AlphaMissense validates each residue (all AM scores < 0.3), (3) cell-free expression produces the protein, (4) cryo-EM verifies the 3D structure matches the design.

              

              
Math → Code derivation (5 steps)

              

                Step 1: Specification
                
spec = {'function': 'ATP motor', 'length': 250, 'folds': 'alpha-beta}

                
The user provides a functional specification: what the nanomachine should do (ATP hydrolysis), its size (250 aa), and required fold (alpha-beta barrel).

              

              

                Step 2: ESM-3 generation
                
seq = ESM-3.v4.generate(spec)

                
ESM-3 v4 (10B params, trained on 100M protein structures) generates a sequence matching the spec. The model conditions on the 3D structure + function tokens.

              

              

                Step 3: AlphaMissense validation
                
all(AM(residue) < 0.3 for residue in seq)

                
Each residue in the generated sequence is checked against AlphaMissense. If any residue has AM > 0.3 (potentially destabilising), the sequence is regenerated. This ensures the protein is stable.

              

              

                Step 4: Cell-free synthesis
                
protein = cell_free_expression(seq, yield='mg/mL')

                
The validated sequence is expressed via cell-free (in vitro) translation — no living cells needed. Typical yield: 1-5 mg/mL in 2 hours.

              

              

                Step 5: Cryo-EM verification
                
RMSD(design, cryoEM) < 2A -> verified

                
Cryo-EM of the expressed protein. If the RMSD between the designed and actual structure is < 2A, the nanomachine is verified and ready for use. 73% of designs pass on first try (2028 vision).

fusion_data_centre(compute_load, reactor) - carbon-negative compute

              
Simulate the 2029 fusion-powered data centre: SPARC reactors provide electricity, AI controller maintains Q > 20, excess heat powers district heating.

            

            ▾
          

          

            

              
              
              
            

            
            

              

                
P_electric = 2 * P_SPARC * eta_thermal; P_compute = P_electric - P_heating; CO2 = -0.1 * P_compute (net negative)

                
Two SPARC reactors provide 2 * 500 MW thermal = 1 GW thermal. At 40% thermal efficiency, that's 400 MW electric. The AI controller runs the plasma at Q > 20 (net energy gain). 200 MW of waste heat powers district heating. Net CO2: negative (fusion produces no CO2 + district heating displaces gas).

              

              
Math → Code derivation (5 steps)

              

                Step 1: Fusion power
                
P_fusion = 2 * 500 MW = 1 GW thermal

                
Two SPARC-class reactors, each producing 500 MW of fusion power. The D-T fusion reaction produces 17.6 MeV per event (3.5 MeV alpha + 14.1 MeV neutron).

              

              

                Step 2: Thermal-to-electric
                
P_electric = P_fusion * eta = 1 GW * 0.40 = 400 MW

                
The thermal-to-electric conversion uses a Brayton cycle with 40% efficiency (advanced supercritical CO2 turbine). 400 MW of electricity for computing.

              

              

                Step 3: Waste heat recovery
                
P_heating = P_fusion * (1 - eta) = 1 GW * 0.60 = 600 MW thermal

                
The 600 MW of waste heat (at ~500C) powers district heating for a city of ~200k people. This displaces natural gas heating, saving ~200k tonnes CO2/year.

              

              

                Step 4: Compute load
                
P_compute = P_electric - P_AI_controller = 400 MW - 5 MW = 395 MW

                
The AI fusion controller uses ~5 MW (1.2% overhead). The remaining 395 MW powers the compute (GPUs + cooling). At 0.01c/kWh, that's $0.95M/day for 395 MW.

              

              

                Step 5: Carbon-negative
                
CO2_net = -200k tonnes/year (displaces gas heating) + 0 (fusion) = -200k t/yr

                
Net CO2: negative 200k tonnes/year. The fusion produces zero CO2. The waste-heat-powered district heating displaces ~200k tonnes of natural gas CO2. The reactor construction is amortised in 2 years (steel + REBCO magnets + concrete).
