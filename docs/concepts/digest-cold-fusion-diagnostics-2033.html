Futuristic Vision (2033): AI-driven cold-fusion diagnostics - autonomous plasma characterisation

AIHPC Editorial Board - extrapolated from SPARC, AI fusion control, plasma diagnostics (2025-2031) · AIHPC Futuristic Vision · 2033-Q2 (extrapolated)

Abstract

This is a futuristic vision, not a real publication. We extrapolate from CFS SPARC (2026), AI fusion control (2028), and high-throughput plasma diagnostics (2030) to predict that by Q2 2033, AI-driven autonomous diagnostics will characterise low-energy nuclear reaction (LENR / 'cold fusion') experiments in real-time, settling the 1989 Fleischmann-Pons controversy.

The vision: a 'plasma diagnostics foundation model' (PlasmaDM v3, 10B params) ingests 12 simultaneous plasma observables (neutron flux, gamma spectrum, tritium count, calorimetry, mass-spec, X-ray, neutron spectroscopy, charged-particle spectrum, IR imaging, UV emission, magnetic probes, acoustic emission) and infers the underlying nuclear reaction rate + branching ratios.

By 2033, 7 of 10 LENR experiments show consistent above-background tritium + heat signatures (>= 3 sigma) when run with PlasmaDM v3 diagnostics. The remaining 3 show only calorimetric anomalies (consistent with electrochemical artefacts). The controversy is effectively settled: LENR effects are real but small, and require hyper-pure Pd-D loading + specific crystal facets.

5-step pipeline: observable ingestion, signal processing, PlasmaDM v3 inference, statistical validation, branching-ratio output.

Math → Code derivation

R_nuclear(t) = PlasmaDM-v3(obs_1(t), ..., obs_12(t)); branching = softmax(R_nuclear)
PlasmaDM v3 (10B params) ingests 12 simultaneous observables + outputs the underlying nuclear reaction rate R(t) + branching ratios (D+D → T+p, D+D → He3+n, D+T → He4+n, etc.).
Step 1: Observable ingestion
O = {neutron_flux(t), gamma_spectrum(t), tritium(t), heat(t), ...}
12 simultaneous observables at 1 kHz: neutron flux, gamma spectrum, tritium count, calorimetry, mass-spec, X-ray, neutron spectroscopy, charged-particle spectrum, IR imaging, UV emission, magnetic probes, acoustic emission.
Step 2: Signal processing
O_clean = filter(O) - background(O)
Each observable is filtered (high-pass for acoustic, low-pass for calorimetry) and background-subtracted using a matched-filter against the reactor's quiescent baseline.
Step 3: PlasmaDM v3 inference
R_nuclear(t) = PlasmaDM-v3(O_clean)
PlasmaDM v3 (10B params, trained on 100M simulated + 1M real plasma shots) infers the underlying nuclear reaction rate R(t) from the 12 observables. Outputs branching ratios for each reaction channel.
Step 4: Statistical validation
significance = R_nuclear(t) / sigma_background > 3 -> detection
Statistical significance: 3-sigma above background = detection. 7 of 10 LENR experiments show 3-sigma above-background tritium + heat (the remaining 3 show only calorimetric anomalies).
Step 5: Branching-ratio output
branching = softmax([R_DD_Tp, R_DD_He3n, R_DT_He4n])
The branching ratios between D+D reactions (T+p vs He3+n) and D+T reaction (He4+n) reveal the underlying mechanism. 7/10 experiments show D+D branching consistent with hot-fusion plasma — suggesting a surface-catalysed D+D mechanism in Pd-D.
