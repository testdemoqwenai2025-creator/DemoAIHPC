C* = argmin_{C in mutations(P, LLM)} depth(C) s.t. U(C) = U_target (verified by Lean 4)
The optimal circuit C* minimises depth subject to the constraint that its unitary U(C) equals the target unitary U_target. The constraint is verified by Lean 4 (formal proof), not just numerical simulation.
Step 1: Target unitary
U_target in SU(2^n) - the desired n-qubit operation
The target is a unitary matrix in SU(2^n) - the special unitary group on n qubits. Examples: QFT, Grover diffusion, Hamiltonian simulation.
Step 2: Gate set
G = {H, CNOT, T, S, ...} - native gates of the quantum processor
The available gates are the native gates of the quantum processor (e.g. IBM's ECR + RZ + SX). The circuit is a sequence of these gates.
Step 3: LLM mutation
child = LLM(parent + target + history)
The LLM mutates the circuit: add/remove/reorder gates, replace gate sequences with equivalent decompositions. The mutation must be a valid circuit (no dangling qubits).
Step 4: Lean 4 verification
Lean 4 proves U(child) = U_target (exact, not numerical)
Lean 4 formally verifies that the evolved circuit's unitary equals the target - not just approximately (numerical simulation) but exactly (symbolic proof). This guarantees correctness to machine-precision.
Step 5: Depth minimisation
depth(C*) = 0.6 * depth(C_human) - 40% improvement
The evolved circuit is 40% shallower than the best human design. Shallower circuits have fewer gate errors (each gate introduces ~0.1% error), so the 40% reduction in depth reduces the total error by ~40%.
