Batch Normalisation

BatchNorm normalises each layer's activations to have zero mean and unit variance (per mini-batch), then applies a learned scale (gamma) and shift (beta). This stabilises training, allows higher learning rates, and acts as mild regularisation (batch noise). BatchNorm was a key enabler of training very deep networks (ResNet).

Math → Code

y = gamma * (x - mu_B) / sqrt(sigma_B^2 + eps) + beta
Step 1
mu_B = (1/m) * sum(x_i); sigma_B^2 = (1/m) * sum((x_i - mu_B)^2)
Compute the mean and variance of the activations over the mini-batch of size m. These statistics are different for each batch — introducing stochastic noise.
Step 2
x_hat = (x - mu_B) / sqrt(sigma_B^2 + eps)
Subtract the batch mean and divide by the batch std. eps = 1e-5 prevents division by zero. The result has zero mean and unit variance (per batch).
Step 3
y = gamma * x_hat + beta
gamma and beta are learned parameters that allow the network to undo the normalisation if it's not helpful. Without gamma/beta, BatchNorm would force every layer to have unit variance — too restrictive.
Step 4
mu_running = alpha * mu_running + (1-alpha) * mu_B
During training, keep a running average of mu_B and sigma_B. At inference, use the running averages instead of batch statistics (since inference may have batch size 1).
Step 5
Reduces internal covariate shift + enables higher lr + mild regularisation
BatchNorm stabilises the distribution of activations across layers, reducing 'internal covariate shift'. This allows 10x higher learning rates and faster convergence. The batch-level noise also acts as mild regularisation (reducing the need for dropout).
