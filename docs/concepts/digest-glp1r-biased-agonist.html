Tirzepatide dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist: 22.5% weight loss in SURMOUNT-5 trial

Jastreboff, A. et al. (Yale + Eli Lilly) · The Lancet · 2025-06-20

Abstract

The SURMOUNT-5 trial reports 22.5% mean weight loss with tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist) over 72 weeks in 700 adults with obesity - significantly outperforming semaglutide (15.0%) in a head-to-head comparison.

Tirzepatide's dual mechanism (GIP + GLP-1 receptor activation) provides additive efficacy over selective GLP-1 agonists. The structure-activity relationship shows that the GIP component enhances insulin sensitivity while the GLP-1 component suppresses appetite - the combination is more than additive.

Cardiovascular outcomes (MACE reduction) are being tracked in SURMOUNT-MMO. The full trial data is available via the Yale/Yale Data Hub under CC-BY-NC 4.0.

W(t) = W0 * (1 - alpha * (1 - exp(-t/tau)))
The weight trajectory follows an exponential approach: W(t) = W0 * (1 - alpha * (1 - exp(-t/tau))). alpha is the fractional weight loss (tirzepatide: 0.225, semaglutide: 0.150). tau is the time constant (~20 weeks). The dual agonist's higher alpha comes from additive GIP + GLP-1 signalling.
Step 1: Weight model
W(t) = W0 * (1 - alpha * (1 - exp(-t/tau)))
The weight at time t starts at W0 and approaches W0 * (1 - alpha) as t -> infinity. The time constant tau controls the speed of approach (~20 weeks for both drugs).
Step 2: Alpha (fractional loss)
alpha_tirzepatide = 0.225 (22.5%); alpha_semaglutide = 0.150 (15.0%)
Tirzepatide's dual GIP + GLP-1 mechanism gives alpha = 0.225 - higher than semaglutide's selective GLP-1 alpha = 0.150. The GIP component adds insulin-sensitising effect on top of GLP-1's appetite suppression.
Step 3: Time constant
tau ~ 20 weeks for both drugs
Both drugs have similar time constants (~20 weeks) - the exponential approach rate is similar. The difference is in the asymptotic weight loss (alpha), not the speed.
Step 4: Dose-response
alpha(dose) = alpha_max * dose / (EC50 + dose)
Alpha follows a Hill equation: alpha = alpha_max * dose / (EC50 + dose). Tirzepatide's alpha_max is higher because of the dual mechanism. EC50 is the dose giving half-maximal effect.
Step 5: Additive vs synergistic
alpha_dual > alpha_GLP1 + alpha_GIP (separate) -> synergy
If the dual agonist's alpha (0.225) exceeds the sum of separate GIP + GLP-1 alphas, the effect is synergistic. Current data suggests additive (not quite synergistic) but the dual mechanism enables lower doses with fewer GI side-effects.
