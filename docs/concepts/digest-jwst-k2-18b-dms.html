JWST detection of dimethyl sulfide (DMS) in K2-18b's atmosphere - potential biosignature

Madhusudhan, N. et al. (Cambridge) · Nature · 2025-04-15

Abstract

JWST NIRISS + NIRSpec transmission spectra of the Hycean planet K2-18b reveal a 3.4-sigma detection of dimethyl sulfide (DMS) at 3.4 micrometres - the first potential biosignature gas detected on an exoplanet.

DMS on Earth is produced exclusively by marine phytoplankton. On K2-18b (a Hycean planet with a possible liquid-water ocean), the DMS detection is consistent with a biological source, though abiotic pathways (photochemistry) cannot yet be ruled out.

Follow-up observations with JWST MIRI are scheduled for Cycle 3 to confirm the DMS detection at 5-8 micrometres and search for additional biosignature gases (O2, CH4, N2O).

Slide each molecular template (DMS, H2O, CH4) across the observed spectrum with Doppler shift v. The cross-correlation peak at v=0 (planet rest frame) indicates detection; peak height = column density.
Step 1: Observed spectrum
F(lambda) = F_star * (1 - delta(lambda) * Lambda(mu))
JWST measures the transit depth delta(lambda) modulated by limb-darkening Lambda. K2-18b is a Hycean planet (H2-rich atmosphere + possible ocean).
Step 2: DMS template
T_DMS(lambda) from HITRAN at 3.4 micron
DMS (dimethyl sulfide, (CH3)2S) has a strong absorption band at 3.4 micron. On Earth, DMS is produced exclusively by marine phytoplankton — a strong biosignature.
Step 3: Cross-correlation
CC(v) = sum_lambda F_w(lambda) * T_w_DMS(lambda + v*lambda/c)
Slide the DMS template with Doppler shift v. Peak at v=0 (planet rest frame) = DMS detection. Peak height = DMS column density.
Step 4: Significance
3.4 sigma at v=0 — candidate detection
The DMS detection is 3.4 sigma — a candidate, not a confirmed detection. Need MIRI follow-up at 5-8 micron to confirm.
Step 5: Biosignature assessment
DMS + H2O + no CO = 'strong' tier (multi-gas disequilibrium + ocean)
DMS + H2O without CO (thermodynamic disequilibrium) on a Hycean planet = 'strong' biosignature tier. Abiotic DMS pathways exist but are less likely on a Hycean world.
